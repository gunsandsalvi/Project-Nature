Struck stone, bone flutes and an invented tongue

Every sound here is made from numbers, not recordings: what things are made of, their shape and size, and how they are struck. Your ear makes the final choice. For each section, tell us which version sounds right, or that neither does.

Struck materials B74 · SND-06

Each clip is the same three strikes (hard, soft, harder) by a quartzite hammerstone. A1 builds the sound from the object's ringing modes; A2 is the cheap one. Tell us, material by material, which sounds more like the real thing, and whether the four are easy to tell apart.

Instruments from their shapes B74 · CUL-10

The flutes' notes come only from the bone's length, bore and holes, and the drums' from the hide's size, tension and weight. Nothing is tuned to a scale.

Bone flute A 22 cm, 8.4 mm bore, five holes spaced evenly by eye

Notes from the bore and holes: 753, 868, 981, 1123, 1314, 1584 Hz; overblown 1506 Hz. Played pitch within 3.3 cents of the worked-out pitch.

Bone flute B 31 cm, wider bore, four larger holes
