What to look for

  • Each material has a short ladder of hand-picked colours, and light chooses the step.
  • A fine dither appears only in a narrow band where two steps meet, and it stays fixed to the surface as you turn.
  • Firelight warms the ground, the rock and the faces nearby in clean steps that fade with distance.
  • People, animals, trees and the cliff top carry a one-pixel dark outline. Where the low sun or the fire catches an edge, it turns bright.
  • The cliff is real geometry: limestone beds of different thicknesses, cracks and long fissures, lichen, water stains, soot above the cave, grass over the rim and scree at the foot.
  • The river bends, narrows and widens, with gravel bars, reeds and a ford. From far away it never gets thinner than a pixel or two.
  • People and animals are small 3D figures posed like sprites, about 10 poses a second, so they turn properly with the camera.
  • Zoom out and the view tilts down into a clean map, then the globe.
  • The cut-away slices the ground open: rock, soil, groundwater and an old hearth with its tools and bones.
