# Research 08: culture, language, belief and history

**Question:** how do games give each people its own language, customs, beliefs and history, made by the world rather than written by hand (`CUL`, `SCP-18`, `SCP-20`)?

## How others do it

- **Naming languages.**
  Martin O'Leary's method, which many games use, builds a light sketch of a language:
  - its sounds and the shapes its syllables may take;
  - its spelling;
  - a stock of word-parts with fixed meanings, so names are built from meanings ("river-stone") and sound like they belong together.

  ([mewo2](https://www.mewo2.com/notes/naming-language/), after Mark Rosenfelder's Language Construction Kit, [library.conlang.org](https://library.conlang.org/blog/?p=41)).
  Vulgarlang does the same, generating the sounds, words for English meanings and a grammar ([Vulgarlang](https://www.vulgarlang.com/how-it-works/)).
- **Customs as data rules.**
  Comme il Faut writes social norms as reusable, recombinable rules over people's traits and relationships (research 07).
- **Histories.**
  - Dwarf Fortress simulates its world's history and lets you browse every civilisation and figure in Legends mode ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_Fortress)).
  - Caves of Qud does the opposite: it generates events first, then explains them after the fact with a state machine and replacement grammars, giving "rich, convincing histories on a tight scope budget" ([GDC 2018](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024990/Procedurally-Generating-History-in-Caves), [paper](https://pcgworkshop.com/archive/grinblat2017subverting.pdf)).
- **Cultural evolution research** (research 07): skills and styles spread by imitation and teaching, drift in small groups, and can be lost, as Henrich's model of Tasmania shows.

## What we take

1. **Each people's language is a naming language in O'Leary's way.**
   - Its sounds, syllable shapes and word-parts come from the seed.
   - Names are made from meanings, so a place named after its river and its stone sounds right in its language (`CUL-17`).
   - New peoples' languages are derived from their parent's, by shifting sounds and swapping words, so related peoples sound related.
2. **Customs, taboos, rites and beliefs are data rules:**
   - over what people do, know and value;
   - created by people's own experiences (a death after eating something becomes a taboo);
   - spread by teaching and talk, as skills are (research 07).

   No custom is written by hand for a people, and none is borrowed from a real culture (`SCP-20`).
3. **History is simulated, as in Dwarf Fortress, never generated after the fact as Caves of Qud does.**
   Our principles require every entry to trace back to what really happened (`PRN-10`, `PRN-15`).
   From Caves of Qud we take only its replacement grammars, for wording myths and stories from the recorded events (research 09).
4. **Myths are people's own explanations of real events:**
   - a lightning fire, a flood, an eclipse;
   - built from their beliefs about causes (`MND-05`);
   - retold, and changed a little each time they pass from person to person.
