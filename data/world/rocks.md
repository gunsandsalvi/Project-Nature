# Rocks

The rocks the land is made of (A5.7, `WLD-09`): each lies in beds, wears away at its own rate where water runs over it, and shows its own look in cliffs and cuts (`PRE-23`).
Softness scales how fast running water wears a rock down, from 0.5 for the hardest to 2 for the loosest (A5.7 step 4); beds are the layers a cliff's face shows, each a thickness drawn from the rock's range (A11.5).
The first region's escarpment shows chalk over sandstone over granite; its river carries gravel and leaves silt (A5.6).

## Chalk

Soft white limestone of the shells of tiny sea creatures, laid in beds with bands of flint nodules between them.
Water dissolves it along its cracks, so caves and rock shelters form in it.
