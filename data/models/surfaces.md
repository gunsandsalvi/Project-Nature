# Surfaces

The ground's surfaces (A11.5, `PRE-20`): each names the looks it is drawn in, one to three, and how a world-fixed noise splits them between its looks, so patches stay where they are and keep their edges at every zoom.
A surface marked `rock` draws in the rock category, whose outlines are finer than the ground's (A11.2).
Ground cover stands on some: stones, eight-faced, 0.05 to 0.6 m across, and tufts of three to five blades, each drawn while it spans a few art pixels, so they thin out one by one as the camera rises (A11.5, `PRE-46`).
Each has a micro-relief, its texture in the light: a world-fixed noise tilting the ground's normal, in octaves from its largest wavelength halving down to its smallest, each fading out as it shrinks below four art pixels, so the texture fades as the camera rises rather than flickering (A11.5).

## Grass

Grass over soil, wherever the ground is gentle: green in leaf, with patches gone to straw where it is drier.
A world-fixed noise of 24 m and 6 m octaves splits the two looks; dry grass takes the noise above 0.25, about a fifth of the ground in spring.
Its relief is clumps of 0.4 to 1.6 m.
Tufts stand in it, one a square metre, lush or dry with the grass round them, and the odd stone.
