# The land presets' tuned numbers

How a land preset's few numbers become its cells (A5.6, `WLD-34`): the shape of its coast and sea, its hills and uplands, its valley, its escarpment's place, its rock beds and its caves.
Changed only in a new rules version, each change logged in `data/TUNING-LOG.md` (`RES-16`); any change moves land, so it needs a new `generator` and `major` too (A5.14).

## Lands

The coast wanders by a noise with a period of a quarter of the island's width, up to a fifteenth of the width in or out; the land rises from the shore over 3 km to its full height, and the sea floor falls from 20 m at the shore to 200 m at the sea's outer edge.
The hills are a noise of 5 octaves from 16 km; the north third stands 60 m higher as uplands, rising over 5 km.
The valley's river rises 3 km inside its coast and runs through points every 4 km, each up to 3 km to one side; its floodplain lies 2 m above the river, and its sides rise over 1.5 km; its stretches run at most 10 km (A5.7 step 5).
The escarpment runs 8 km from the island's middle.
The top rock bed is 24 to 40 m thick and the one below 20 to 30 m.
Caves and shelters open within 15 km of the valley, at least 1.5 km apart, 8 to 30 m deep, seven in ten caves dry, and the first camp's within 3 km of the river; two springs rise 20 m out from the foot of each cell of the face with a cave or shelter.
Reeds take a fifth of the river's and floodplain's cover, grass three fifths of the floodplain's, and bare rock three fifths of the face's; trees are 20 to 80 years old.
Marsh forms on a floodplain that drains over 200 km² and slopes under 0.3°.
The air cools 6 °C for each 1,000 m above its weather cell's mean land.
