# Kindling project file: gaps found by walking through scenarios

An adversarial review of `PROJECT.md` (1,445 lines), looking for completeness. I read the whole file and then walked through six scenarios step by step. At each step I asked what exactly happens, and whether the file says. I did not change any file in the project.

**Scenario key**
- **S1** Your first hour: install, first world, first screen, learning the controls, first intervention, closing and coming back.
- **S2** A world's whole life: generation, the first band, the signature moments in milestone order, the long middle, the endings.
- **S3** God powers used in strange, extreme or repeated ways, and doing nothing for centuries.
- **S4** Failure and stress: extinction, explosion, stagnation, a slow phone, full storage, a damaged save, updates, rewinding across an update, many branches.
- **S5** One discovery traced through every layer, from physics to the chronicle to the experiment that measures it.
- **S6** One person's life, from birth to death and after.

**Counts:** 11 High, 24 Medium, 10 Low.

**Severity:** *High* means it would cause wrong building, rework or a broken promise. *Medium* means a decision will be made by accident, or there will be real confusion. *Low* means it is worth settling when convenient.

Line numbers are given as "l." and refer to `PROJECT.md` as it is now.

## High

### H1. Where you look changes what happens

- **Found in:** S3 (watching from the globe for centuries), S4 (rewinding), and PRN-08's own example.
- **Type:** contradiction. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** PRN-08 (l. 281–285), MND-14 (l. 944), WLD-12 (l. 606), WLD-13 (l. 608), PRN-11 (l. 287–291), TIM-03 (l. 512), GOD-08 (l. 468), RES-05 (l. 1257), PLT-05 (l. 1229), TIM-13 (l. 533).

**The problem.** MND-14 runs minds near "your attention" in full and the rest in a simpler form. WLD-12 takes the land down to the metre "where you look". So the camera decides how people and animals are simulated. A simpler mind will not make exactly the choices a full one would. PRN-08, though, says history is "fully determined by its seed and your interventions", and the camera is not an intervention: GOD-08 and the glossary record only your powers. This leads to three problems:

1. The same seed gives different histories depending on where you looked.
2. Cloud runs have no camera. PRN-08's own example (open a cloud world on the phone and "watch year 41 happen exactly as reported", l. 284) fails as soon as your looking sharpens a mind that the cloud ran in simple form, or the reverse.
3. TIM-03 says the director never changes events. But it pulls you to live moments, which moves your attention, which changes the detail, which changes events. Comparing branches (MOM-10, TIM-13) then mixes the effect of your change with the effect of where you happened to look.

"Attention" itself is never defined. It could be the centre of the camera, everything on screen, the person you follow, or the places the director flags. At globe zoom, PRN-11's check ("what you're watching is simulated in the same detail whatever the load", l. 291) would demand full detail everywhere.

**Why it matters.** Rewind, branching, cloud experiments, replays and the reproducibility check all rest on PRN-08. This choice shapes the whole simulation core, so it must be settled before MIL-01.

**Your decision:** what decides how much detail each person gets, and may that change history?
- (a) Detail never changes outcomes. The simple and full versions must give identical results, and only drawing and sound are skipped far away. This is safest for PRN-08 but saves little work.
- (b) Where you look is recorded alongside your interventions and replayed exactly, and cloud runs use a fixed rule (for example, "attention follows the director"). The file must then say that the same seed watched differently gives a different history.
- (c) The world sets detail by itself (for example by crowding, or a fixed share per band) and never by the camera.

### H2. What a person is when nobody is looking

- **Found in:** S3 (doing nothing for centuries), S4 (population explodes), S5 (who notices the discovery), S6 (a far-away life).
- **Type:** hidden question and contradiction. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** MND-14 (l. 944), MND-11 (l. 914–929), TIM-12 (l. 520–524), TIM-02 (l. 500–510), VIS-11 (l. 132), PRN-07 (l. 227–231), SCP-04 (l. 379), CUL-02 (l. 967), MOM-02 (l. 139), BIO-14 (l. 810), PRN-13 (l. 267–271), PRN-01 (l. 215–219), PRE-09 (l. 1121–1123), PRE-10 (l. 1125), WLD-12 (l. 606), MOM-06 (l. 143), GOD-12 (l. 441–444), GOD-04 (l. 446–451), MND-15 (l. 946), WLD-04 (l. 562), PRN-10 (l. 261–265).

**The problem.** MND-14 says far-away minds run as "habits, and knowledge held by the group as a whole". The file never says what such a mind can still do. Its own examples need far-away people to invent things: the overnight summary's "on the coast, someone began drying fish" (l. 524), live moments for firsts that happen "elsewhere" (l. 502), and fire-making spreading band to band while you watch from above (l. 132).

- If simple minds can't notice accidents and try things (MND-11), a world watched from the globe, or run overnight, never invents anything.
- If they can, through some "chance of discovering X", that needs a list of things that can be discovered. That is a tech tree, which PRN-07 and SCP-04 forbid.

There are other clashes:
- "Knowledge held by the group" contradicts CUL-02 (knowledge lives in heads) and MOM-02 (the craft dies with particular stoneworkers).
- Do BIO-14 (every death has a cause), PRN-13 (every choice can be explained) and PRN-01's check (every discovery traced to experiences) hold for simple minds?
- PRE-09 ("tap a find to see who made it") and PRE-10 (family trees) need far-away people to keep their names, families and life histories.
- Animals: WLD-12 simulates them "in patches". But MOM-06 needs individual bold wolves passing tameness on to their pups, GOD-12 sends a dream to one wolf, and GOD-04 blesses a herd. Which animals are always individuals?
- Scale: MND-15 sets no population cap, and WLD-04 talks of millions. If everyone is always an individual, speed falls as the population grows. If groups can be merged into summaries, PRN-10 forbids inventing their histories when you zoom in.

**Why it matters.** This decides whether history moves at all when you're not watching. It also decides whether experiments, which nobody watches, test the same minds you see in play.

**Your decision:** what always exists for every person and animal, and can a far-away mind discover something new?
- (a) Everyone is always a full individual with their own memories and skills. Far away they decide more cheaply, but they can still notice, try and learn.
- (b) Individuals always exist, with body, family, skills and beliefs held per person. Far-away people only use and pass on what they know, and new discoveries happen only at full detail.
- (c) Far-away groups become summaries, and people are rebuilt when you look. This is cheapest, but PRN-10 and CUL-02 would have to change.

### H3. The starting state is mostly undefined

- **Found in:** S1 (the first world), S2 (the first band), S6 (the first people's lives).
- **Type:** gap and contradiction. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** BIO-02 (l. 774–781), BIO-03 (l. 783), SCP-01 (l. 328–331), VIS-06 (l. 97), PRN-01 (l. 215–219), MND-11 step 1 (l. 924), MIL-03 (l. 369), MIL-04 (l. 370), VIS-11 (l. 128), MND-05 (l. 870), glossary "Belief" (l. 1420), MND-18 (l. 875–881), BIO-08 (l. 785), CUL-07 (l. 998), WLD-24 (l. 600), RES-08 (l. 1241).

**The problem.** BIO-02 is the only list of what people start with, and PRN-01 forbids anything beyond it. It leaves out most of what a first day needs:

- **Fire.** Does each band start with a fire burning? MND-11 step 1 and MIL-03 assume bands are already keeping fire, and VIS-11's band "has lost its fire". Yet lightning and wildfire, the only sources BIO-02 allows, arrive with weather in MIL-04. In a temperate region with no clothing (BIO-02), a band without fire would mostly die in its first winter.
- **Knowledge.** "Gathering, scavenging, some ambush hunting" and feeding a fire need beliefs about what is edible, what burns, where water is and which animals are dangerous. Yet BIO-02 says "Beliefs: none are set in advance", and the glossary defines a belief as "a person's idea of what causes what". That is a contradiction.
- **Memories and maps.** Do adults start out knowing their home range (MND-18), each other, and their own past? BIO-08 says traits are shaped by childhood food and illness, but that childhood was never simulated.
- **Families.** "Family bands" means someone decided who is whose partner, parent and sibling, and whether the bands are related, although CUL-07 says family rules emerge.
- **Who is there.** Ages, sexes, and how many children are in each band of 15–30.
- **Words.** Which meanings do the "few dozen" words have, what do they sound like, and do all bands share them? VIS-06 says "a handful of words" (l. 97), while SCP-01 and BIO-02 say "a few dozen".
- **When and where.** The season at year 0, and whether the bands share one cave.

**Why it matters.** The starting state is an input to every experiment (RES-08 lists the "starting kit"), and anything loaded silently breaks PRN-01. With too little, bands die in their first year. With too much, the early discoveries are pre-loaded.

**Your decision:** how are the first people made, and what exactly do they know?
- (a) A short "prehistory": run the bands for some years before year 0 with only the kit, so their local knowledge, memories and families are real.
- (b) A full written list in BIO-02 of every starting belief, skill, word and relationship, and the fire, for your OK.
- (c) A bare start with no local knowledge at all, accepting heavy early losses.

Also: do bands start with a fire? Yes, no, or some of them?

### H4. Nothing defines how the game spots and names what emerges

- **Found in:** S5 (the live moment, the overlay, the chronicle and the measurement of one discovery), S2 (bands forming and splitting).
- **Type:** gap (a system that other items imply but nothing defines). **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** PRN-07 (l. 227–231), TIM-02 (l. 500–510), PRE-08 (l. 1119), PRE-07 (l. 1117), TIM-13 (l. 533), CUL-23 (l. 1006), PRE-36 (l. 1137), CUL-06 (l. 996), PRE-05 (l. 1111–1113), RES-03 (l. 1263–1269), H1.

**The problem.** The simulation never names discoveries (PRN-07), yet much of the game must spot them and name them:
- "firsts" for the director (TIM-02);
- live moments such as "someone has made fire for the first time" (PRE-08);
- "who knows which skill" on the overlay (PRE-07);
- counts of discoveries, languages and beliefs when comparing branches (TIM-13);
- peoples (CUL-23), and when a dialect becomes a language (PRE-36);
- named institutions (CUL-06), bands forming and splitting (TIM-02), and eras (PRE-05).

No item says how any of this works. Some of the open questions:
- Two people's fire-making may be different sequences of actions, such as a hand drill and a bow drill. Are they one skill?
- Is a first the first in the world, or the first for this people? Does an independent rediscovery count?
- These recognisers must use our words ("fire-making"), but PRN-07's check is "an automated search of the decision-making logic" for exactly those words. Where is the line? If the director uses them to slow time, and that moves your attention (H1), they reach the simulation.

**Why it matters.** The director, live moments, overlays, branch comparisons and every experiment's measurements depend on this. It is a whole system, and no item defines it.

**Your decision:** how does the game recognise and name new things?
- (a) By outcome only. A "first" is the first time someone causes a new kind of change in matter, or a new sequence of actions spreads, and English names come from the writer AI.
- (b) A separate written catalogue of notable outcomes. It is used only for display and measurement, is walled off from the simulation, and is excluded from the PRN-07 search.
- (c) Both: general novelty detection for live moments, plus the catalogue for experiments and overlays.

### H5. Experiment 1's pass criteria can't be checked as written

- **Found in:** S5 (how an experiment measures the discovery).
- **Type:** ambiguity. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** RES-03 (l. 1263–1269), RES-09 (l. 1243), RES-02 (l. 1261), RSK-01 (l. 1337–1340), WLD-06 (l. 566), TIM-14 (l. 537), M4.

**The problem.** RES-03 is "Decided as starting values", and RES-09 says criteria are fixed before the run and never changed. But several criteria can't be measured until choices nobody has made are made:
- "Discovery": one lucky sharp flake, a belief, or a skill they can repeat?
- "Can do it": at what success rate? And "adults" from what age?
- "Differ widely", "noticeably more often", "much rarer" and "does not spread": by how much?
- "Small, isolated" groups against "large, connected" ones: defined how?
- "Routes": how is a discovery classed as an accident or as tinkering?
- Do worlds where everyone dies before year 500 count as failures?
- "500 simulated years" is counted in world years, and their length varies widely between worlds (see M4).

**Why it matters.** Loose words let a result be read to fit after the fact, which is exactly what RES-09 exists to stop. This is the project's first and biggest test (RSK-01).

**Your decision:** who turns these words into exact numbers and definitions, and do you approve them before the run?
- (a) The implementation plan sets them, and you approve them before the run.
- (b) The independent reviewer (RES-11) sets them before anyone sees results.
- (c) They are added to RES-03 itself, as "Done when".

### H6. No rule for failed experiments or tuned values

- **Found in:** S5 (the experiment), S2 (milestones in order).
- **Type:** gap. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** RES-09 (l. 1243), PRN-05 (l. 233–237), VIS-05 (l. 200–203), RSK-01 (l. 1337–1340), RES-11 (l. 1247), PRC-02 (l. 1303), PRC-10 (l. 1323–1325), SCP-16 (l. 365).

**The problem.**
- RES-09 stops anyone changing the criteria. Nothing stops anyone changing the simulation and re-running on the same 100 seeds until it passes, which has the same effect.
- PRN-05 says every value has a real-world source. Many values for minds and culture have none: how fast a hunch strengthens, how often curiosity strikes, how fast memories fade, how imperfect copying is. They will be tuned, and the file doesn't say against what.
- The file doesn't say what happens when an experiment fails. PRC-10's milestone checks include "the independent review of experiments" and "the report", not a pass. Can a milestone close on a failed experiment, and who decides?

**Why it matters.** "Experiments that can fail" is the research promise (VIS-05, PRN-05). Without these rules, any experiment can eventually be made to pass.

**Your decision:** what happens when an experiment fails, and how are values with no real-world source set? These options can be combined.
- (a) Log every attempt. After any change, re-run on fresh seeds, and after a set number of failures, raise it with you.
- (b) Tune on one set of seeds and test once on a separate set kept back for that. List every tuned value and what it was tuned against.
- (c) A failed experiment holds the milestone until you choose: redesign, accept a weaker claim, or drop the claim.

### H7. After an update, can the past still be replayed?

- **Found in:** S4 (an update mid-world; rewinding across an update), S2 (MOM-07 "see the hunt"), S1 (opening an experiment world).
- **Type:** hidden question. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** PRN-08 (l. 281–285), PLT-09 (l. 1222–1225), TIM-06 (l. 528–531), MOM-07 (l. 144), MOM-10 (l. 147), PLT-05 (l. 1229), RES-15 (l. 1293), PLT-08 (l. 1220), PRC-11 (l. 1327).

**The problem.** PRN-08 promises identical replays only "under the same version of the rules". PLT-09 keeps the past "exactly as it was" and runs new time under new rules. But many features re-run the past:
- watching an old moment again, including "you tap it and see the hunt" (MOM-07);
- rewinding and carrying on without changes (TIM-06);
- opening any cloud experiment world "at any moment of its history" (PLT-05, RES-15);
- importing an export that "opens exactly as it was" (PLT-08).

Each needs the rules that made it. A new build comes out "whenever something you can see or try has changed" (PRC-11), so there will be many versions. The file doesn't say whether old versions are kept.

PLT-09 also says a branch started before an update runs under the new rules. That branch then differs from the original for two reasons, your change and the new rules, and MOM-10's comparison can't tell which caused what.

**Why it matters.** This decides what "rewind" and "replay" really offer, and a large part of how the app is designed.

**Your decision:** how is the past kept after an update?
- (a) Keep every rules version in the app. Any past moment and any old experiment replays exactly, and you can branch under the old rules or the new ones.
- (b) Keep the past as a recording. You can watch and inspect it, but anything re-run uses today's rules.
- (c) Keep today's rules only. After an update, old moments can be seen only through saved data (chronicle, archaeology, cards), and old experiment worlds will play out differently on the phone.

### H8. New layers arriving in old worlds

- **Found in:** S4 (an update mid-world), S1 (your first world, started early in the project).
- **Type:** gap and contradiction. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** PRN-14 (l. 301–305), MAT-14 (l. 714–717), MAT-16 (l. 721–728), PLT-09 (l. 1222–1225), WLD-09 (l. 577–584), MAT-09 (l. 664–667), GOD-05 (l. 409–417), PRN-10 (l. 261–265), PRE-25 (l. 1059–1061), RSK-12 (l. 1386–1389).

**The problem.** The project grows by adding materials, species, laws and whole layers (PRN-14, MAT-14, MAT-16), and worlds are meant to be long-lived (PLT-09). But world generation (WLD-09) makes everything at once. When an update adds tin ore, a new disease, a new animal family or a new personality trait to a world that is already running, where does it come from? Tin appearing in rock people have already dug, or a germ appearing from nowhere, is "something from nothing" (MAT-09, GOD-05). The cut-away view (PRE-25) would then show tin in rock layers that formed before tin existed in that world.

RSK-12 rates the effects of updates as "impact low". Yet this hits your very first world: a world started at MIL-02 has no fire, no animals and no weather.

**Why it matters.** It decides whether your worlds survive the milestones, and whether a world's past stays honest.

**Your decision:** what happens to an existing world when the rules gain new things?
- (a) Back-fill: new things are generated into old worlds as if they had always been there, and the join is marked on the timeline.
- (b) Old worlds keep the layers they were made with, and new layers appear only in new worlds. This needs option (a) of H7.
- (c) Worlds made before a named milestone (say MIL-06) are expected to be thrown away, and long-lived worlds start after it.

### H9. Inborn tendencies and drives miss what society grows from

- **Found in:** S6 (family, relationships, injury and care), S2 (MOM-11 rivals, then in-laws).
- **Type:** gap. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** MND-21 (l. 893–895), MND-07 (l. 887), MND-17 (l. 856–860), BIO-12 (l. 800), BIO-13 (l. 804–806), BIO-15 (l. 822), BIO-03 (l. 783), BIO-06 (l. 838), CUL-07 (l. 998), CUL-16 (l. 971), CUL-21 (l. 1000), CUL-04 (l. 975), CUL-10 (l. 1014), MOM-11 (l. 148), MOM-04 (l. 141), PRN-01 (l. 215–219).

**The problem.** MND-21 reads as a complete list: a taste for sweet and fat, wariness of bitter, fear of snakes, heights and the dark, parent–child attachment, the urge to imitate, and suspecting a hidden someone. Nothing social goes beyond parent and child. Several tendencies that research treats as inborn are missing, and the promised stories need them:
- incest avoidance and attraction to a mate (who pairs: BIO-15, CUL-07; marriages between bands: CUL-16, MOM-11);
- favouring kin, empathy, and care for the sick (BIO-13's band carrying the injured hunter);
- returning favours, fairness and punishing cheats (sharing and exchange, CUL-21; "who owes whom", MND-18);
- favouring one's own group, and defending territory (MOM-11's fight over a valley);
- shared attention and pointing, and a readiness to learn words (CUL-04: language from a few dozen words);
- rhythm and music (CUL-10, MOM-04).

MND-07's drives also leave out pain, though BIO-12 says "willow bark eases pain" and BIO-13 has injuries. Without pain, how does anyone learn that fire burns? And with 45–120 founders (BIO-03), real heredity (BIO-06) and no incest avoidance, inbreeding will harm their health.

**Why it matters.** MND-17 says a people's intelligence comes from "small, well-understood learning mechanisms", and these tendencies are that foundation. If the list is closed, social stories may never emerge. If it isn't, AI agents will add tendencies on their own, each one shaping every world, which is exactly what PRN-01 guards against.

**Your decision:** is MND-21 a closed list?
- (a) Closed, so everything social must be learned. This is the purest choice and the riskiest.
- (b) Extend it now from research, with sources, as Proposed items for your OK, each with a comparison run (RES-10).
- (c) Let the plan propose an addition only when an experiment needs it.

### H10. Weather and disaster powers have no physical budget

- **Found in:** S3 (powers used in extreme and repeated ways).
- **Type:** hidden question and ambiguity. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** GOD-05 (l. 409–417), GOD-02 (l. 421–428), GOD-10 (l. 457), PRN-03 (l. 247–251), MAT-09 (l. 664–667), WLD-15 (l. 618).

**The problem.** GOD-05 says powers "never create anything from nothing", with "no limited supply of power", and limited only by what nature could do. Still open:
- **Source.** When you "bring" a storm (l. 411), is an existing storm moved, or is one built where none would form? If it is built, its water and energy come from nowhere (MAT-09). If it is moved, another place loses its rain.
- **Strength.** How hard is "a hard winter", and how dry is "a dry year"?
- **Size.** How large a "region" (GOD-02) can you draw round? Drawing round the whole world would be changing the climate, which GOD-02 rules out.
- **Repetition.** Each event is checked on its own (GOD-05's check). So a once-a-century drought every year for 50 years passes, though nature would never produce that run. Is PRN-03's test ("events the world could produce on its own") about each event, or about the pattern?
- **Disasters.** Can you set off the biggest quake a fault allows twice in a row? Does an eruption you trigger use up the magma, so the next one comes later?

**Why it matters.** These are the rules of your main lever, and they decide whether your world's history stays natural.

**Your decision:** what limits weather and disasters beyond "possible here and now"?
- (a) Nothing more: anything possible may be repeated at will.
- (b) Powers work like fortune: they shift the odds within the place's real range of weather, with a stated cap and a size limit, so extremes stay rare.
- (c) A physics budget: storms are moved or built from real moisture and energy nearby, and quakes and eruptions use up stored strain or magma, so repetition limits itself.

### H11. "Doubles a chance" has nothing to act on

- **Found in:** S3 (fortune used repeatedly and stacked).
- **Type:** contradiction and ambiguity. **Severity:** High.
- **Items:** GOD-04 (l. 446–451), MAT-06 (l. 694), GOD-05 (l. 409–417), PRN-03 (l. 247–251), PRN-08 (l. 281–285).

**The problem.** GOD-04 says a blessing at most doubles a chance, so a hunt with a 10% chance becomes 20%. But a hunt's outcome comes from physics, bodies and minds (MAT-06: "the physics decides the outcome"). There is no single "chance of success" in the simulation to double; it is the result of many small events. The file doesn't say where fortune acts (aim, an animal's attention, a stumble, whether a germ takes hold, conception), or how anyone checks the "at most double" limit.

Also undefined:
- **Stacking.** If you bless a person, their family, their band and the place they hunt, is that 16 times? What if you bless the hunters and curse the herd they hunt?
- **Renewal.** With no limit on power (GOD-05), a blessing can be renewed forever, giving permanent double luck.
- **Fertility and health.** What exactly is doubled: the chance per month, or per illness?

**Why it matters.** It decides whether fortune is a gentle nudge to nature or a hidden luck layer on top of it, which PRN-03 forbids. It must also be reproducible (PRN-08).

**Your decision:** where does fortune act, and how do blessings combine?
- (a) Fortune nudges the small random parts of the simulation for its target, and tests check that the overall effect never goes beyond double or half.
- (b) Fortune adds its own luck roll on top of the physics. This is simple, but it is a rule above nature.

For combining: only the strongest applies, or all apply but the total is capped at double, or they multiply.

## Medium

### M1. Passed experiments are never re-run

- **Found in:** S2 (milestones in order), S4 (the world grows).
- **Type:** gap. **Severity:** Medium.
- **Items:** VIS-12 (l. 136), MAT-15 (l. 719), PRC-10 (l. 1323–1325), RES-07 (l. 1271–1280).

**The problem.** VIS-12 says each signature moment "becomes a long-term test", but nothing says when experiments run again. MAT-15 re-runs the reality checklist after every addition, and there is no equivalent for experiments. PRC-10's merge checks list "the tests", and it isn't clear whether these include experiments. The milestone checks list only the review of experiments.

**Why it matters.** MIL-04 adds predators, weather and disease. If bands now die before they learn to chip stone, Experiment 1 quietly stops passing and nobody notices.

**Your decision:** when do passed experiments run again?
- (a) All of them, at every milestone.
- (b) A small, fast version before each merge, and the full version at milestones.
- (c) Only when a change touches a system the experiment depends on.

### M2. Milestones leave out systems the early experiments need

- **Found in:** S2 (the signature moments in milestone order).
- **Type:** gap and contradiction. **Severity:** Medium.
- **Items:** SCP-16 and MIL-01 to MIL-07 (l. 365–373), RES-03 (l. 1263–1269), MOM-01 (l. 138), MND-11 (l. 924), MOM-02 (l. 139), WLD-21 (l. 645), BIO-05 (l. 808), MAT-16 (l. 721–728), RES-07 (l. 1280), PRE-31 (l. 1083–1088).

**The problem.**
- Experiment 1 (MIL-02) runs for 500 years and measures "adults", and loss in small, isolated groups against large, connected ones. That needs births, ageing, deaths, pairing, children who learn, and several bands in contact. MIL-02 lists none of these, and no milestone mentions the body systems of section 8 at all.
- MOM-01 (tested in MIL-03) happens "in a hard winter", to a band keeping fire from lightning, with wolves at the edge of camp (MND-11). Weather and animals arrive in MIL-04. MIL-01 and MIL-04 both claim seasons.
- MOM-02 (MIL-04) needs a fever, but microbes and disease (WLD-21, BIO-05) appear in no milestone.
- MAT-16 says "each milestone adds a layer". Read one to one, fibres, hides and joining (clothing, spear points fixed to shafts) arrive with "Words and beliefs", and pigments with "The whole world". MIL-04's "hunting arms race" would have no fixed points.
- MOM-07, MOM-09 and MOM-10 are "checked at milestone reviews" (l. 1280), but the file doesn't say which reviews.
- PRE-31's visual review runs "at every milestone", though MIL-01 has only "a basic phone viewer".

**Why it matters.** The plan will place these by accident, and the early experiments may run in worlds that lack what they test.

**Your decision:**
- (a) Name in this file which body, disease, social and matter systems each milestone brings, and when each feature-type signature moment is checked.
- (b) Leave all sequencing to the plan, and say so here.

### M3. Experiment worlds and play worlds may not be the same kind of world

**Your decision:** should these be decided in this file, or left to the plan, with your review of the first build?

### L5. Tides on worlds without moons

- **Found in:** S2 (generation).
- **Type:** contradiction. **Severity:** Low.
- **Items:** WLD-26 (l. 634), WLD-06 (l. 566).

**The problem.** WLD-26 says tides are "set by the moons", and WLD-06 allows worlds with no moons. The sun also causes tides, about half as strong as the moon's on Earth. Read literally, a moonless world loses its tides, and with them the shellfish beds exposed at low tide.

**Your decision:** confirm that the sun's tides count, so moonless worlds still have weaker tides.

### L6. The estimate of how many people a world can feed doesn't add up

- **Found in:** S4 (the population explodes).
