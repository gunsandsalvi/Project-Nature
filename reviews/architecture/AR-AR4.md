# AR4: P6 (A9 Culture and society, A10 The player) and P8 (A13 Sound)

### 1. [blocker] A10.2, A10.5, A5.8 Four weather powers do what the world never does by itself
Problem: A10.2 says every power calls "the function a natural event calls", but A5.8's weather has no natural version of four of them. Natural rain comes only from storms moving hourly with the wind, natural droughts are season-long wetness anomalies over whole regions, and A5.8's "any cold snap" term is fed only by acts. So rain ("a storm held over that weather cell for up to a day"), drought (a mask with no rain at all), cold snap (3 days at the record low) and flood ("a front held over the upper valley for a day, strength from the top of the storm range") happen only when you act, and the flood even takes the strongest storm instead of a natural draw. `GOD-05`'s check, and A10.5's twin test and two-sample size test, cannot be built for these four. A5's 84-byte climate record also lacks the stormiest season's storm count, the dry-spell lengths and the cold depths that A10.2's conditions and A10.5's check read; A10.5 checks against the "worst season" where `GOD-05` says "worst year"; and A10.2 (3 m) and A5.8 (5 m) disagree on the strike radius.
Fix: A5.8 adds three natural events per weather cell, drawn at climate rates (`WLD-30`), each with its own size distribution: a stalled front or shower (6–36 h), a blocking high (no rain over up to 50 km for 1–15 days) and a cold outbreak (1–3 days, at most down to the season's record low). Calibration stores, per weather cell and season, the stormiest season's storm count, the longest dry spell and the deepest outbreak. Acts call these with only place, time, stretch and length: rain is a shower born over the cell and stalled up to a day; a flood is a stalled front over the upper valley, its strength drawn as a natural stall's; a drought is a blocking high; a cold snap is an outbreak at the record low, as `GOD-02` sets. A10.2's table cites A5.8; A10.5 says "worst year"; the strike radius (5 m) lives in A5.8 only.
Part: P3 (A10.2 and A10.5 then cite it)

### 2. [major] A10.4, A10.5, A5.8, A3.3, A14.8, A12.5 Traces of your acts reach the world and the recognisers
Problem: A10.5 rests `GOD-06` and `GOD-07` on "no type in the lower crates holds an act id" and "no event names you"; other sections break both. A5.8's `Storm { …, cause: Natural | Act(ActId) }` puts an act id in `kd-world`, so an act-made storm hashes differently from its natural twin and A10.5's twin test fails by design. A3.3's `roll` and A10.4's `roll_outcome` return `turned_by_fortune` to the rule that rolled, so code in `kd-people` and `kd-life` holds a flag meaning "the god did this" and must log `fortune_turned` itself. A14.8 logs act results "as events linked to the act" in the shared history log, which A12.5 hands to the recognisers, and those live in `kd-player` beside `record`, so no crate boundary keeps acts from them.
Fix: (1) `Storm` drops `cause`; A10.6's links in `PlayerState` are the only record of what an act made. (2) The fortune roll returns only the outcome; the cluster context appends each turned roll (subject, purpose, second) to a buffer that only `kd-player` drains at the barrier, and `kd-player` logs `fortune_turned`. (3) `act_result` and `fortune_turned` go only to `acts.log` and the `player` chunk, never to the `EventSink` stream that minds and recognisers read; a test with 200 acts asserts no act kind reaches either. (4) The recognisers leave `kd-player` (finding 6).
Part: P6 (one-line follow-ups in P3 A5.8, P1 A3.3, P9 A14.8)

### 3. [major] A13.9 The murmur ships a neural voice in the app where strung syllables would do
Problem: ONNX Runtime (about 15 MB), a voice of up to 45 MB and the recordings (4 MB) already pass A15.3's 50 MB cap on the APK, which is committed to `dist/` and fetched by raw GitHub link (A15.4). B76 measured Piper voices of 64 MB (Welsh: kept the most sounds, but one male speaker and an unclear licence) and 79 MB (English: kept 13 of 20 sounds); 45 MB needs 8-bit weights the owner has not heard, and two single-speaker voices would pass GitHub's 100 MB limit. It also adds a native runtime, `Voice.kt`, `Request::Speak` and `voiceResult` (none in A2.2 or A2.5), plus about 250 MB and a minute of a core at every new world. Every world made in the browser has no murmur, though the web build is how each alpha reaches the owner (A15.4) and the murmur arrives at `MIL-03`. Formant synthesis, however muffled, is no way out: the owner called it terrible (B76), `SND-03` asks for the natural-sounding voice chosen by ear, and telling child, woman, man and elder apart, or anger from grief, needs the 1–4 kHz detail that muffling removes, at close camp where 4 voices are heard singly.
Fix: make A13.9's own fallback the design. `tools/voices.py` renders in the cloud, once per voice version, every half-syllable of the voice's kept sounds (each onset with its vowel, consonant + liquid or glide included, and each vowel with its coda), stressed and unstressed, two takes each, cut at vowel midpoints from carrier phrases: about 4,000 units, about 5 MB of ADPCM a voice, one file per voice beside the code on both targets (each under the web host's 15 MB). When a world is made, Rust strings each phrase of A9.3's plan from these units, joined in the vowel with 15 ms crossfades, its length and pitch contour set by the overlap-add pitch shift that singing needs anyway (A13.10, brought forward to `MIL-03`): well under a second, no cache file, no Kotlin, no request. Browser-made worlds get their murmur too, and the cloud can run any voice, including larger multilingual ones that keep more invented sounds (B76's flag). At `MIL-03` the owner compares 10 strung phrases with the same phrases spoken whole by the model; only if the strung ones fail does the on-phone model return, with the APK cap raised by the owner.
Part: P8

### 4. [major] A10.7, A4.14 Signs reach the director only at the barrier, after they are over
Problem: the director learns of signs only at the next barrier (A10.7, A4.14), up to 15 game minutes late, while the display runs only 3 frames behind the world (A4.11). At camp speed (8 game minutes a real second) a window passes in under 2 real seconds and a 2-minute stalk in a quarter of one, so the director almost always hears of a stalk after it ended: `PRE-08`'s own example, a predator stalking someone you follow, and the 10-minute hostile sighting can slow time only for the aftermath, never "before an outcome" (`TIM-02`). Both events are named only in A10.7: A7.9 logs no stalk and A9 never defines `hostile_sighting`.
Fix: add `Director::on_step(signs: &[SignEvent], env: &Env) -> Option<SpeedAsk>`, called by the sim thread after every step with the sign events that step logged, carried on the per-step output that already brings sound events (A4.13). A sign is scored only from facts at its own second, never from what followed. `SpeedAsk` carries `from: GameTime`, and kd-app slows when display time reaches that second; the world is ahead of the display by the lead, so nothing looks ahead in the world. Moments from recognisers stay at barriers. A7.9 logs `stalk_started { predator, prey }`; A9.9 logs `hostile_sighting { band, other }`.
Part: P6 (A4.14 adds the per-step call)

### 5. [major] A10.1, A10.3 vs A4.8 Acts apply at the next barrier here, at the next second in A4
Problem: A4.8 settles a conflict by applying an act at second T + 1, phase Act (weather acts joining the next hourly step), and recording T + 1 in `acts.log`. A10.1 step 3 and A10.3 still apply acts "at the next barrier", re-check them "since up to 15 game minutes may have passed" and let them fail ("she woke", "the storm moved on"); A5.8, A14.7 and A14.8 also say "the next barrier". Builders get two rules, a failed re-check breaks `GOD-11`'s Done when (the world carries out every act the ring offers), and "she woke" contradicts A10.3's own rule that an awake person's dream waits for their next sleep.
Fix: in A10.1, `apply` runs at T + 1, phase Act (A4.8): lightning re-aims the storm's next strike; a dream goes into a sleeper's slot, or waits in `PlayerState` and is delivered at the first barrier of an awake target's next sleep; fortune enters `FortuneBook`; a quake or eruption is set off. Rain, storm, drought, cold snap and flood join the next hourly step with their conditions taken as the ring saw them, so nothing the ring offered fails. `ActRecord.applies` is T + 1; drop "she woke" and "the storm moved on". A5.8, A14.7 and A14.8 cite A4.8.
Part: P6

### 6. [major] A10.7, A10.9 vs A4.14, A2.2, A12.3–A12.5, A14.2 The director and recognisers have no single interface or home
Problem: A4.14 has a pure `assess(&World, at) -> DirectorOut` with the budget clock in kd-app; A2.2 has `director(&World) -> SpeedRequest`; A10.7 has a stateful `Director` (`on_barrier`, `on_frame`, `tapped`, `skip`) that keeps the clock itself. A12.5 puts the recognisers in `kd-player`, which A10.1's module list omits. A10.9's check cannot run: `tools/layers.py` reads `cargo metadata` (A15.1), which sees crates, not modules, and "fails on `&mut`" would reject the director's own `&mut self`. Director state goes to `view.bin` (A10.9) or `ui.bin` (A12.4), and A14.2's world folder lists neither. The budget comes with live moments at `MIL-02` in A10.10 but at `MIL-06` in A4.14. The ring is `ViewRequest::Powers` within 2 ms (A10.1) or `Query::PowersAt` within 1 ms (A12.3).
Fix: a new crate `kd-book` holds the recognisers and the director, depends only on `kd-core`, `kd-data` and `kd-view`, and declares `BookQuery` (read-only facts, including `act_linked(uid) -> bool`), which `kd-sim` implements; it cannot name `kd-player`'s types, so `GOD-07` is held by the crate graph and the `&mut` rule goes. A10.7's stateful `Director`, with real time passed in `Env`, is the one interface; A4.14 and A2.2 cite it. View state, the director's included, goes in `ui.bin`, listed in A14.2. The budget arrives with live moments at `MIL-02`. The ring is `Query::PowersAt`, and A12.3 allows it 2 ms.
Part: P6 (A4.14, A2.2, A12.5 and A14.2 cite it)

### 7. [major] A13.3, A13.13 The 32 slots carry about twice 32 sounds' work by `MIL-05`
Problem: A13.13 assumes every kind of slot costs at most 1.5 strikes, but B74 measured the flute at 1,150 sounds per core against about 4,500 for a shaped-noise strike: about 4 strikes. A bed is a wash plus up to 2 grains (3 strikes), the chorus 3 sung lines, a sung voice an overlap-add. A13.14's own worst case, a storm over a festival, comes to about 66 strike units (place 6, voices 4, loud 2, camp 8 singles and 4 beds 20, music about 29, reverb 2, stage 3): at B74's 0.35% of a small core per sound, about 23% mean and 36% at the 99th percentile, over A13.13's 20% and 35% and at A16.2's 25%. The cap falls only after the `MIL-03` check, which has no music.
Fix: cost each generator kind in strike units in `data/tuning/sound.md` (strike, step, wash or phrase 1; bed 3; flute 2, with at most 8 harmonics and one noise band; sung voice 2; chorus 6; reverb 2; stage 3), and cap the mix at 56 units (about 20% of a small core by B74); over it, the quietest singles of the dearest layer join its bed. Rerun the phone check at `MIL-05` with the festival in a storm, lowering the cap to 24, then 16, by the same rule as at `MIL-03`.
Part: P8

### 9. [major] A9.6, A9.4 Customs lapse when unused, and a new band starts with none
Problem: a band's answer is "the one taken in more than half its cases of the last 5 years, once there are 3; until then it has none". For rare questions (captives, a marriage ending, succession, strangers) a band often has fewer than 3 cases in 5 years, so its custom vanishes, though `CUL-06` keeps a custom "after its reason is forgotten". A9.4's split also starts the new band with no answers, against `CUL-06`'s "a new band starts with its parent's answers"; since a people's customs are those most of its bands hold, every split dilutes them, which weakens A9.16's "different peoples" test.
Fix: an answer, once formed, stands until a different answer is taken in more than half of at least 3 cases in the last 5 years; with fewer cases it stays. At a split the new band copies its parent's answers and case rings.
Part: P6

### 10. [major] A9.4, A9.7, A9.9, A9.11, A9.15 Six institutions lack a rule that can be coded
Problem: (1) Nothing ever chooses to take a captive in, and a captive is killed only by chance in a fight or a chief's punishment, so almost every question-11 case is "kept" and that custom is the same in every world: scripted in effect. (2) A9.9 says when a raid is offered and how the odds are judged, but not which band is the target, so `CUL-31`'s "drawn by villages" has no rule. (3) `CUL-26`'s priest is succeeded by "the one they taught, most often their child" and lives by the role; A9.11 uses question 8 and gives the priest no food. (4) `CUL-30` lets leavers bring close kin and "join a village of their people"; A9.4 has neither. (5) A festival is "kept by its sign, most often the full moon", with no rule for which sign. (6) "The top sum leads until a rival's passes it by a fifth" reads as an automatic change, while `CUL-22` changes leaders only by a won challenge.
Fix: (1) A year after capture, the captor family's head chooses among taking in (scored by opinion of the captive, a marriage, the band's need for hands), killing (anger, revenge, hunger; dark-tagged, `CUL-08`) and keeping; that choice is the case. (2) Target score for each band the decider knows within 2 days' walk: known food, stores and herds × the odds ÷ distance × (1 − share who are kin), × 1.5 against a people below −20. (3) The successor is the apprentice with most rite experience, a child first on ties; the priest is fed by gifts under A9.8's specialist rule. (4) The leaving families' adult children and parents are offered to come too; joining a village of their people within a day's walk is an option beside founding a band. (5) The sign is the people's known sign whose expected day falls nearest the gathering's usual dates, else that season's full moon. (6) Passing by a fifth makes a rival eligible to challenge; the leader changes only by a won challenge, death or leaving.
Part: P6

### 11. [major] A9.4, A9.12 Style drift departs from `CUL-12`, whose Done when then cannot pass
Problem: A9.4 starts a new people "one step and its scale one note" from its parent, but `CUL-12` says a new people "starts with its parent's". A9.12 runs random drift and drift toward the people met most side by side (up to two shifts in 25 years), where `CUL-12` has one shift about every 25 years, toward a people in contact if there is one. Even so, `CUL-12`'s Done when (any two peoples differ in at least 2 of the 6 choices at Year 150) fails whenever a people formed after about Year 120: at one shift per 25 years, two peoples 10 years apart rarely differ in 2 choices, and drift toward the people met most, usually the parent, pulls them back together.
Fix: follow `CUL-12`: a new people starts with its parent's style and scale; each year one choice shifts with chance 1/25, toward the people met most in the last 5 years if any, else at random. Raise a Proposed change to `CUL-12`'s Done when, "any two peoples that have been separate for 50 years or more differ in at least 2 of the 6 choices", list it under Proposals awaiting confirmation (`PRC-07`), and test that wording until the owner decides.
Part: P6
