The art lane then checks every part, looks at it on its preview sheet and fixes what is wrong.

Prompt:
You are making the first person for a game set in the Stone Age, about 12,000 years ago. The game draws a sharp 3D world whose every surface wears a pixel-art texture of 64 texture pixels a metre: a standing adult is about 105 texture pixels tall and a face about 14. Every figure is one skeleton with a body of parts skinned to it; code poses the bones about 10 times a second, and shape keys give each person their build, age and sex. The attached picture is the family sheet the owner accepted: read the proportions of the adults (the first row's fourth figure) and the clothes (a tunic to mid-thigh with sleeves to mid-forearm, leggings, and soft boots to mid-calf with a fur trim). Never copy its flagged things.

Write people.py, a Blender 4.0 script that builds the skeleton, the body and the garments below with the helper module kit.py in this folder, and saves people.blend. First read kit.py and kitmath.py in full: their docstrings say what each function does and which rules it keeps. Start people.py with:

import math, os, random, sys
sys.path.insert(0, os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__)))
import kit

then kit.start(), the skeleton (kit.armature), the parts (kit.Part with armature= and atlas=, built with sleeve(), shaped with paint()), the bend test (kit.pose), and kit.finish(sys.argv[-1]).

and clavicle.R, upper_arm.R, forearm.R, hand.R, thigh.R, shin.R, foot.R, toe.R with x negated.

THE BODY: eight kinds of part, each a kit.Part with armature=arm and atlas=skin, where skin = kit.Atlas("person_body", 2.0, 2.0), group "person": head, torso, upper_arm.L, upper_arm.R, forearm.L, forearm.R, hand.L, hand.R, thigh.L, thigh.R, shin.L, shin.R, foot.L, foot.R (name each part body_head, body_torso, body_upper_arm.L and so on). Each is one or more sleeves (wrap=False: a figure's texture is drawn to its layout, so each ring spans its own true circumference) with weights for every ring.

How the parts join without a gap, the rule that matters most: where two parts meet at a joint (neck, elbow, wrist, knee, ankle), both have a ring there at the same place, of the same size, with the same weights (half each bone), the last ring of one and the first of the other, both open (caps None); then they bend together and never part. Keep every ring's numbers in one table for its chain (a list of rings, each its height or place along the bones, its two radii and its weights), and build both parts from that table, so the shared ring is defined once. Shape keys change rings through the same table, so the shared ring moves alike in both parts. Where a limb meets the torso (shoulder, hip), the limb's first ring sits at the joint inside the torso, deep enough that no gap opens when it turns.

Weights: a ring at a joint is half each of the two bones; the rings next to it, three quarters to their own bone and a quarter to the other; the rest wholly their own bone. The torso's rings: from its bottom to the waist hips, through the belly spine, the chest rings chest, the neck ring neck; the head's rings neck at its bottom then head.

Sizes, as radii across (side, x) and front to back (y) at rest, for an average adult; 10 to 12 sides for the torso, head, thighs and shins, 8 for the arms, hands and feet; place the seam where it shows least: the back of the torso and head (seam = math.pi / 2 for a sleeve running up), the inner side of each limb (for a limb running down: seam 0 on the left limb and math.pi on the right), the underside of a hand and a foot.
- torso, from a bottom ring at the crotch (z 0.80, closed with a flat cap) up: hips (z 0.88: 0.17, 0.11), waist (1.02: 0.14, 0.095), belly (1.10: 0.145, 0.10), chest (1.25: 0.16, 0.11), upper chest (1.33: 0.17, 0.105), shoulders (1.38: 0.19, 0.09), neck base (1.42: 0.07, 0.06), neck (1.47: 0.055, 0.055, shared with the head).
- head, from the neck ring up: jaw (1.50: 0.062, 0.075), cheeks (1.55: 0.072, 0.092), brow (1.60: 0.074, 0.094), crown (1.635: 0.055, 0.07), top (1.648: 0.03, 0.04) closed with a flat cap; the face is drawn later on its front (-y), about 14 texture pixels tall, so keep the front smooth: no nose or ears in the mesh. Then paint("hair", test) the faces of the scalp: above a hairline that runs at the brow (z 1.60) in front and down to the nape (z 1.50) at the back.
- upper arm, along its bone from the shoulder joint: shoulder (0.05), upper (0.047), above the elbow (0.04), elbow (0.036, shared).
- forearm: elbow (shared), widest (0.038, a quarter down), wrist (0.027 across, 0.02 deep, shared).
- hand, a mitten with a thumb: wrist (shared), palm (0.045 across the palm, 0.016 thick), knuckles (0.044, 0.013), finger ends (0.036, 0.01) closed with a flat cap; the palm faces the thigh at rest; the thumb its own sleeve of 3 rings (0.012 to 0.009), from the palm's front edge, pointing forward and down, its end closed, weighted to the hand.
- thigh, from the hip joint (inside the torso): top (0.085), middle (0.075), above the knee (0.058), knee (0.05, shared).
- shin: knee (shared), calf (0.052 across, 0.058 deep, a quarter down), lower (0.04), ankle (0.032, 0.036, shared).
- foot: ankle (shared), then down and forward along the foot's bones to the toes: instep (0.042 across, 0.05 tall), ball (0.047, 0.026), toes (0.04, 0.018) closed with a flat cap, its sole flat at z 0; the heel reaching about 5 cm behind the ankle.
The body is about 1,300 triangles in all.

SHAPE KEYS, on every body part and garment, each a sleeve's keys={...} giving the radii (and where needed the points) at 1: build_thin (girth of torso and limbs about 0.85, face slightly narrower), build_stout (belly and waist 1.25, chest, hips and limbs 1.12, a fuller face), sex_female (shoulders 0.9, waist 0.92, hips 1.08, breasts as the chest rings' front fuller by 1.5 to 2 cm, arms and legs 0.93, hands and feet 0.92, a narrower jaw), age_child (the head 1.25 times larger about its neck ring, shoulders and hips 0.85, limbs and torso slimmer, the belly a little rounder; how tall a child is comes from the skeleton's bone lengths, which the game sets), age_elder (limbs and chest 0.92, the belly softer and lower, the neck thinner). Keep each key's texture stretch within the line.

THE GARMENTS, three parts on the same skeleton, each a shell a little outside the body (1.5 to 2.5 cm out, looser where cloth hangs), with the same bones' weights as the body under it and the same shape keys, each with its own atlas, group "person", role hide with role fur for trims; mark each as a garment with ob = part.done(); ob["kit_layer"] = "garment":
1. garment_tunic, atlas kit.Atlas("person_tunic", 2.0, 2.0): a pullover hide tunic: a body from the shoulders down to mid-thigh (z 0.62), where its hem hangs free as a skirt (its lowest rings weighted three quarters hips and a quarter to the nearer thigh, so the legs move under it), a neck opening at the neck base, and two sleeves over the upper arms to mid-forearm (each sleeve its own sleeve, starting inside the tunic's body at the shoulder); a fur trim (a ring band of role fur) at the hem and the sleeve ends.
2. garment_leggings, atlas kit.Atlas("person_leggings", 2.0, 1.0): hide leggings: two tubes from inside the tunic (z 0.85) down over the thighs and shins to the ankle, 1 cm outside the body.
3. garment_boots, atlas kit.Atlas("person_boots", 1.0, 1.0): two soft hide boots of the moccasin kind, each one sleeve from mid-calf (z 0.30) down over the ankle and the foot to the toes, 1 cm outside, its sole flat, a fur trim band at its top.

THE BEND TEST: kit.pose(arm, "bend_test", turns) with the knees bent 60 degrees (the shin's foot moving back), the hips 30 degrees forward, the elbows 60 degrees (the forearm moving forward), the neck 15 degrees down; find each turn's sign by checking where the bones end up. The check measures stretch in this pose too.

Rules every part keeps: real size in metres; material slots by role only (skin, hair, hide, fur); texture stretch at most 1.5:1 on every triangle at rest, with each shape key at 1 and in the bend test; every vertex weighted by one to four bones; pieces of one atlas never overlap (kit.Atlas places them; make the atlases large enough).
