HOW THE ENGINE SHOWS THE GROUND
A texture pixel (a texel) is shown about 2 x 2 screen pixels at every zoom. The zoom has bands; each band halves the texels a metre: band 0 has 64 a metre, band 1 has 32, band 2 has 16, band 3 has 8, band 4 has 4, band 5 has 2, band 6 has 1. The camera looks 37 degrees down, so the ground is foreshortened: every texel is shown only about 0.6 as tall as wide on the screen. A big surface has three tiles, each 256 texels across at its first band: near (4 m across, 64 a metre, serves bands 0 and 1), middle (16 m, 16 a metre, serves bands 2 and 3) and far (64 m, 4 a metre, serves bands 4 to 6). Each band has its own level drawn as pixel art for that distance, with bolder marks and fewer of them: never the level above averaged. Several versions of each tile are made from your pictures by code (quilting), so more than one picture of the same material helps.

WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE NEAR TILE IN THE GAME (picture 2 against picture 1)
Your camera view is crisp: round, domed cobbles, each with a lit top at the upper left over a darker body and a thin dark rim, dense small gravel between them, each pebble clear. The flat tile, once the game lays it on the ground, comes out as flat ovals about half as tall as wide, with smeared tops, mushy gravel and none of the dark rims. Measured on 540 x 540 pixels: your camera view has contrast 8.8 and 27 small marks per thousand cells; the game's view of the flat tile has contrast 6.4 and 20. The colours match.

HOW

Keep each raw picture the tool returned, unchanged, in originals/ (copy the file as it is, never convert or resave it, so its provenance data stays) and say which original is which.
