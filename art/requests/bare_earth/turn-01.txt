HOW THE ENGINE SHOWS THE GROUND
A texture pixel (a texel) is shown about 2 x 2 screen pixels at every zoom. The zoom has bands; each band halves the texels a metre: band 0 has 64 a metre, band 1 has 32, band 2 has 16, band 3 has 8, band 4 has 4, band 5 has 2, band 6 has 1. The camera looks 37 degrees down, so the ground is foreshortened: every texel is shown only about 0.6 as tall as wide on the screen. A big surface has three tiles, each 256 texels across at its first band: near (4 m across, 64 texels a metre, each 1.6 cm, serves bands 0 and 1), middle (16 m, 16 a metre, each 6.25 cm, serves bands 2 and 3) and far (64 m, 4 a metre, serves bands 4 to 6). Several versions of each tile are made from your pictures by code (quilting), so two different pictures of the same material help.

THE PICTURES WITH THIS MESSAGE
1. camera-1.3-bare_earth.png: your own camera view from the sheet at the phone's true size (one picture pixel is one screen pixel), about 4.2 m of ground across. This is what the game must deliver up close.
2. earth-near.png: your near tile from the sheet. 3. earth-middle.png: your middle tile from the sheet (16 m). 4. sheet-1.3-bare_earth.png: the whole sheet.

THE JOB: FOUR PICTURES
a) earth-near-A.png and earth-near-B.png: the near tile, 256 x 256 texels, 4 m across, that reads in the game's view like picture 1.
 - Cracks 1 texel wide (dark, with the lit lip on one side), plates of dry crust between them about 40 to 90 texels across; plates as irregular as your tile's, never regular polygons. Because the game foreshortens every texel to 0.6 as tall as wide, draw the plates about 1.5 TIMES TALLER than wide in the flat tile, so that on the screen they look as round as in picture 1.
 - The crumbs and grit of the sheet: small crosses and specks of 2 to 3 texels, a few paler crusts, a few darker hollows; no stones (they are pieces of their own). The palette of the sheet: #B68A58 #C69B67 #D2AD79 #A7794C #91633E #755438.
 - No broad swells, no big patches: the tile repeats every 4 m and the middle and far tiles carry the broad variation. Keep the average tone even over the tile.
 - Flat light, no shadows cast, no scale sticks, no tint. A and B: different arrangements of the same ground, the same character and average colour.
b) earth-middle-A.png and earth-middle-B.png: the middle tile, 256 x 256 texels, 16 m across (each texel 6.25 cm), from your middle tile (picture 3), which is signed off: keep its design, the finer web of thin dark cracks over plates about 10 to 20 texels across with broader, quieter paler and darker patches under them. Draw the plates and patches about 1.5 times taller than wide here too, cracks 1 texel wide, no single-texel speckle, no diagonal pattern, no large dark or light patches that would repeat every 16 m. A and B are different arrangements, the same average colour as the near tile.

HOW
Save each as a PNG 1024 x 1024 pixels with every texel one flat 4 x 4 block. Your image tool returns about 1254 x 1254 pixels with cells that are not all the same width and a picture that does not wrap: that is expected and my code's job, not yours. NEVER bin a drawing because of its size, its grid or its seams; bin only a drawing that is itself wrong (a wrong palette, a gradient, something painted on that I said not to). Keep each raw picture the tool returned, unchanged, in originals/ and say which is which. Draw with your image tool, never with code.
