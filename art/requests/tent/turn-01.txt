This conversation is one piece: 16.4, the cone hide tent (Kindling, a Stone Age world simulation for phones). The first picture with this message is its signed-off sheet, the standard; the second is the sheet's "hide's surface" picture enlarged, the third its "true size on the phone" row. I build the tent as 3D parts in Blender: the cover as ELEVEN separate hides (seven in a lower ring, four in an upper ring, each cut and sewn to the next), ten poles, a lashing, a door flap and a ring of 24 stones. What I need from you this turn are the TEXTURES the hides and the stones wear, drawn as pixel art at the size the game really shows them.

THE SIZE THE GAME SHOWS (this matters more than anything on the sheet)
A texture pixel (a texel) is 1/64 of a metre, 1.56 cm, and the game draws it about 2 x 2 screen pixels up close. The tent is 3.8 m across, so about 240 texels. One hide is 1.5 to 2 m wide: 100 to 130 texels. The hide-surface picture at the bottom of the sheet is 1 m across, so it is 64 texels wide in the game: its dapples of 4 to 8 cm are 3 to 5 texels, its hair strokes 1 texel wide. Its stitches are about 8 cm long (5 texels). A stone is 20 to 40 cm: 13 to 26 texels across, so a stone is one small lump with a few speckles on it, not a picture.

HOW ONE-OFF DETAILS ARE CARRIED (so you know what the textures do NOT have to do)
- The hides' different tones (the sheet has light, middle and dark hides side by side) are made by the part: each hide is this one texture darkened by a baked number, so draw the LIGHTEST hide of the tent, one quiet dappled mid-light hide, and never a dark one.
- Light and shadow, the darker hem and the lit upper cone, are baked into the parts too. Paint no light from one side.
- The seams are carried by the parts' edges: along every edge of a hide I put a band 4 texels (6.25 cm) wide that wears a "seam strip" of the texture, so the strip must be drawn by you. It is the sewn seam seen from one side of it.

JOB 1: THE HIDE, hide-A.png and hide-B.png, two different tiles
A tile of 128 x 128 texels (2 m across). Pixel art with every texel an exact flat block of 8 x 8 pixels in a 1024 x 1024 canvas.
- A deer or reindeer hide's surface seen from above in flat light, as in the sheet's surface picture but at game size: soft stepped dapples (3 to 6 texels across, edges stepping like pixel art, never smooth) of two tones on a mid-light ground, the dapples drawn in a few short strokes of hair running DOWN the picture (the tile is laid on the cone with the picture's up-down running down the slope), strokes 1 texel wide and 3 to 6 long, inside the lighter and darker patches.
- Palette: the sheet's hide colours. Ground mostly #C99960 (about 55%), light dapples #E0B578 (about 20%), darker dapples #AA7848 (about 20%), a few darker flecks of #805532 (about 5%). Never #563A25 and never a gradient.
- Quiet: contrast like the sheet's surface picture seen from a phone. No stitches, no seam, no holes, no scars, no veins, no painted designs, no light from one side. The hide fills the tile edge to edge: it is a surface, not a skin laid on a ground.
- Seamless left to right and top to bottom, as best you can.
Hide-B has the same character and a different arrangement.

JOB 2: THE SEAM STRIP, hide-strip.png
The same canvas (1024 x 1024, 8 x 8 pixel texels, 128 x 128 texels). Only its FIRST 4 TEXEL COLUMNS (the left 32 pixels) matter: they hold the strip; fill the rest of the canvas with plain hide ground #C99960 and nothing else. The strip is read like this: its left column, column 0, is the very edge of the hide where the seam is; columns 1 to 3 are the hide going away from the seam, 6 cm of it. The neighbouring hide wears the same strip as a mirror image across the seam, so the two together make the seam: the edge in the middle, running stitches crossing it.
- The strip repeats exactly every 8 texels downward: draw one 4 x 8 pattern and repeat it 16 times, identical, all the way down.
- In each repeat: ONE stitch, a pale thong (#E0B578) one texel tall and three texels wide, from column 0 to column 2 (so together with the neighbour's it is 6 texels, about 9 cm, as on the sheet), with a dark end (#563A25 or #805532) at column 3 where it goes into the hide; the other seven rows of the repeat show the seam's edge in column 0 as a dark line (#563A25) with the hide's edge shadowed in column 1 (#805532) and the plain hide (#AA7848 and #C99960) in columns 2 and 3.
- It is your drawing: if you think the sheet's stitch reads better some other way at this size (a shorter stitch, a pair, a knot), draw that, and tell me why. Keep it to the first 4 columns and the 8-row repeat.

JOB 3: THE STONE, stone-A.png and stone-B.png, two different tiles
A tile of 64 x 64 texels (1 m across), every texel an exact flat block of 16 x 16 pixels in a 1024 x 1024 canvas. Nothing here is a hide: it is the skin of a river stone seen flat. The stone is worn by all 24 stones of the ring, each 13 to 26 texels across, so the tile is wrapped over a small lump; the stone's shape, its lit top and its dark underside come from the part, so draw only the surface: a quiet grey (#85877F as the ground, about 60%) with speckles of one texel or two in #AAA99E (about 20%) and #62675F (about 15%), a few fine darker veins one texel wide and 4 to 10 long, at most two or three small pale lichen patches of 2 to 4 texels (#AAA99E). Never regular, no cracks that run across the tile, nothing countable, no pattern, no colour other than those three greys. Seamless as best you can. Stone-B the same character, a different arrangement.
